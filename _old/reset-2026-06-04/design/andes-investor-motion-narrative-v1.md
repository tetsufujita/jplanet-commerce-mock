# Andes Investor Motion Narrative v1

This document defines the creative source of truth before any implementation. The goal is not to copy a reference site. The goal is to make Andes feel like a visionary infrastructure company by turning its business model into a motion narrative that can become:

- a 45-60 second investor film in Remotion
- a scroll-scrubbed web experience
- 6 styleframes for design approval
- reusable pitch deck and social teaser assets

## 1. Creative Thesis

Andes turns the messy reality of LATAM commerce into infrastructure that AI agents can use.

The visual story must show real commerce friction being transformed into a system:

```
orders + SKU + WhatsApp + PIX + NF-e + PRC + customs + logistics + tax + local entities
  -> Andes platform
  -> agentic commerce infrastructure for Latin America
```

The site should not feel like:

- a consumer fintech app
- a Shopify storefront
- an AI chatbot product
- a generic B2B SaaS landing page
- a reference-site clone

It should feel like:

- an investor-grade operating company
- a cross-border commerce intelligence system
- a future protocol company built from real market operations
- quiet, inevitable, specific, and technically literate

## 2. Investor Reading

The first 10 seconds must answer three investor questions:

1. Is this bigger than a commerce website?
2. Does Andes have a wedge into the market already?
3. Is there a path from operating company to infrastructure/protocol company?

The first viewport should therefore communicate:

- scale: Latin America, not one store
- wedge: Japan/Korea to Brazil commerce operation
- moat: regulatory, tax, logistics, local entity, and merchant operation
- endgame: AI-agent-readable infrastructure, not an app UI

## 3. Tone

The tone is investor cinematic, not consumer playful.

Use:

- paper, navy, ink, restrained grey
- crimson only as a pinpoint signal
- map lines, document fragments, protocol nodes, ledger-like typography
- real operational artifacts as proof
- slow confident motion, not playful bounce

Avoid:

- purple/pink SaaS gradients
- fake browser chrome
- fake metrics
- smiling office-worker stock imagery as the main proof
- cartoon robots
- app screenshots in the hero
- retail CTAs near infrastructure claims

## 4. Core Visual Motifs

### 4.1 Trade Corridor

A single Japan/Korea -> Brazil corridor line. It is not decorative. It is the spine of the company.

Visual behavior:

- starts as a thin crimson point in Japan
- travels across a quiet navy/paper map field
- lands in Sao Paulo
- leaves behind small operational fragments: SKU, order, invoice, customs, logistics, PIX

### 4.2 Friction Fragments

Commerce friction should appear as layered fragments, not cards:

- WhatsApp order snippet
- product SKU line
- PIX payment token
- NF-e / ICMS / LGPD / CDC labels
- PRC / SISCOMEX / ANVISA / BACEN labels
- shipping label / EMS bridge / customs checkpoint
- local entity chain

These fragments should feel like documentary evidence, but abstracted enough to avoid looking like a shopping site.

### 4.3 Andes Platform

The platform is the heavy layer. It should visually absorb and organize the fragments.

Visual behavior:

- scattered fragments align into columns
- columns lock into a layered architecture
- Layer 1 is a thin agent surface
- Layer 2 is a deeper, denser platform layer
- the platform becomes the primary object on screen

### 4.4 Protocol Graph

The endgame appears as a graph only after the operating wedge is understood.

Visual behavior:

- multiple trade events become nodes
- nodes connect into rails
- rails become an interface that global AI systems could call
- do not over-name the protocol in public copy

## 5. 60-Second Film Structure

### Scene 1: Opening Declaration

Duration: 0-7s

Purpose: establish ambition without sounding like vaporware.

Frame:

- off-white or deep navy field
- one quiet map-scale line
- Tokyo / Sao Paulo anchors
- large declarative type

Copy direction:

- "Building the new economic foundation of Latin America."
- Japanese equivalent from homepage SSOT may be used on ja pages.

Motion:

- type rises line by line
- corridor line draws once
- crimson appears only at the endpoints

### Scene 2: The Friction

Duration: 7-16s

Purpose: show why the market needs a new infrastructure layer.

Frame:

- fragments enter from different depths
- WhatsApp, SKU, tax, customs, payment, delivery, entity labels
- no app-card composition

Motion:

- fragments drift in with small timing offsets
- some fail to align at first
- the screen feels complex but controlled

Investor meaning:

- LATAM commerce is not just checkout
- the hard part is physical, legal, fiscal, and operational

### Scene 3: Operating Wedge

Duration: 16-25s

Purpose: prove Andes is not a slideware protocol company.

Frame:

- J-Planet appears as the first deployment
- Japan/Korea products -> Brazil consumer demand
- CNPJ / Sao Paulo / local operating evidence can appear as restrained labels

Motion:

- one real order path travels through the fragments
- the path resolves instead of breaking

Investor meaning:

- Andes is already entering the market as a merchant/operator
- the company owns the hard operational surface

### Scene 4: Layer 1 / Layer 2

Duration: 25-36s

Purpose: make the strategic architecture legible.

Frame:

- Layer 1: purchase agent, thin and replaceable
- Layer 2: logistics, tax, law, customs, ERP, accounting, fintech
- Layer 2 is visually heavier and more valuable

Motion:

- agent surface glides over the platform
- platform nodes continue running underneath
- global AI logos are not needed; represent them as external call points if used

Investor meaning:

- Andes is not trying to beat foundation models
- Andes is building the infrastructure those models need in LATAM

### Scene 5: Platform Expansion

Duration: 36-48s

Purpose: show the roadmap without turning it into a timeline slide.

Frame:

- Phase 1 cross-border B2C
- Phase 2 BR domestic A2A
- Phase 3 ERP/B2B/factoring
- Phase 4 fintech

Motion:

- the initial corridor becomes multiple routes
- platform modules appear from left to right
- each module is compact, not card-heavy

Investor meaning:

- the wedge expands naturally into a market infrastructure company

### Scene 6: Common Infrastructure

Duration: 48-60s

Purpose: close on the category Andes wants to own.

Frame:

- trade events become protocol graph
- Andes mark or wordmark appears with calm finality
- CTA for investor/partner conversation

Motion:

- graph simplifies
- visual noise disappears
- one final sentence remains

Copy direction:

- "Built from the market up."
- "The infrastructure layer for Agentic Commerce in Latin America."

## 6. Six Styleframes

These are the approval frames. No implementation should begin until these frames are visually strong.

### Frame A: Hero / Corridor

What it shows:

- map-scale Japan/Korea -> Brazil corridor
- huge declarative statement
- no commerce UI

Success criteria:

- feels premium without ornament
- investor immediately reads "regional infrastructure"
- crimson usage is tiny and intentional

### Frame B: Friction Field

What it shows:

- fragmented operational labels and documents
- commerce complexity as a visual field

Success criteria:

- looks specific to Brazil/LATAM commerce
- does not look like a generic data dashboard
- still has readable hierarchy

### Frame C: Operating Wedge

What it shows:

- one commerce path resolving through J-Planet-owned operations
- legal/local operation proof restrained in labels

Success criteria:

- proves Andes is operating in market
- does not become a storefront
- evidence feels credible and factual

Note: If the homepage SSOT continues to keep J-Vita off the public homepage, this frame uses J-Planet only.
J-Vita may be reserved for investor-only deck variants, not the public homepage.

### Frame D: Two-Layer Architecture

What it shows:

- Layer 1 agent as a thin access layer
- Layer 2 platform as dense infrastructure

Success criteria:

- investor sees where the moat is
- not a cute diagram
- not a chat UI

### Frame E: Expansion Rails

What it shows:

- corridor expands into modules/rails
- cross-border commerce becomes domestic A2A, ERP/B2B, and fintech rails

Success criteria:

- roadmap feels inevitable
- no fake traction metrics
- modules have clear visual weight

### Frame F: Protocol Close

What it shows:

- commerce events becoming a common graph/interface
- final Andes mark and investor CTA

Success criteria:

- feels visionary, not vague
- resolves visual complexity into calm
- leaves a memorable final image

## 7. Web Translation

The website should be the scrollable version of the film.

### Web Section Mapping

1. Hero Corridor
   - based on Frame A
   - first viewport, minimal copy, no UI screenshots

2. Friction Field
   - based on Frame B
   - scroll reveals the market complexity

3. Operating Wedge
   - based on Frame C
   - shows Andes as merchant/operator, using evidence carefully

4. Layer Architecture
   - based on Frame D
   - explains Layer 1 vs Layer 2

5. Expansion Rails
   - based on Frame E
   - shows phased growth as infrastructure, not a timeline slide

6. Protocol Close
   - based on Frame F
   - investor-oriented close

7. Contact / Investor CTA
   - quiet final section
   - `ir@andes.global` and partner/careers links

### Scroll Behavior

Use scroll-scrub only where it explains transformation:

- corridor line draw
- fragments aligning into platform
- Layer 1 separating from Layer 2
- rails expanding
- graph simplifying into the close

Do not scroll-scrub:

- every paragraph
- every card
- decorative background motion
- random image parallax

Reduced motion fallback:

- show each styleframe as a static completed state
- replace scrub transitions with simple fades
- keep all text readable without motion

## 8. Remotion Direction

Remotion is the source film, not an afterthought.

Composition:

- `AndesInvestorFilm`
- 1920x1080
- 30fps
- 45-60s target duration

Asset strategy:

- SVG for maps, routes, architecture, labels, protocol nodes
- Next/Web can reuse the SVG primitives
- avoid video-heavy backgrounds at this stage
- use generated or photographed assets only where they prove operations

Motion system:

- deterministic timeline
- scene-based sequences
- easing: calm, cubic, confident
- no springy consumer-app bounce
- no carousel

Reusable primitives:

- `TradeCorridor`
- `FrictionFragment`
- `ComplianceRail`
- `LayerStack`
- `ProtocolGraph`
- `InvestorCaption`

## 9. Copy Discipline

Copy must sound like a company building infrastructure, not pitching a consumer feature.

Good direction:

- "Built from the market up."
- "The infrastructure beneath Agentic Commerce in Latin America."
- "From merchant operations to protocol rails."
- "A local operating wedge for a continental infrastructure layer."

Avoid:

- "Shop smarter"
- "Download the app"
- "Start shopping"
- "AI shopping assistant"
- "The future of ecommerce is here"
- "10x faster" or any invented metric

## 10. Open Decisions

These need confirmation before visual production:

1. Public use of J-Vita
   - Current homepage SSOT says J-Vita is not shown on the homepage.
   - This narrative can work with J-Planet only.

2. Regulatory detail level
   - Labels like PRC / SISCOMEX / NF-e are visually powerful.
   - Public copy should not over-disclose sensitive positioning.

3. Investor CTA tone
   - Option A: "Investor relations"
   - Option B: "Talk to Andes"
   - Option C: "Build with Andes"
